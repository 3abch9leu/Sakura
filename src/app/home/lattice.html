Day 0.0 · simulation

An interconnected geometric structure inspired by nature and Tunisian–Japanese heritage. Its empty spaces become growing cells, while hidden irrigation channels and integrated water-storage compartments distribute water passively.

Technical legend

  • Cultural geometric framework. Asanoha (hemp-leaf) triangular lattice in treated wood, joined by eight-point-star connectors from Tunisian mashrabiya.
  • Integrated planting cells. Cells with a flat floor hold a removable terracotta insert, growing medium and room for roots.
  • Open shading and ventilation cells. Pointed cells stay open for light and air, or carry asanoha fins and sakura blossoms that cast patterned shade.
  • Concealed gravity-fed channels. 4 mm channels inside the struts, routed only downward or level from one supply tube at the top.
  • Distributed water storage. A waterproof compartment in the floor beam of each planting cell, filled through a labyrinth inlet sized by the AI.
  • Capillary delivery. A cotton wick lifts water from the compartment into the roots, so soil takes only what it can hold.
  • Drainage. Each compartment overflows down its own strut to a covered tray and jar at the base.
  • Optional monitoring. A level probe and a small station. The structure works without them.
